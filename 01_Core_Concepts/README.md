# Semantic Fidelity Project — Core Concepts

A. Jacobs | Reality Drift Framework | 2023–2026

This directory contains the core concept papers for the Semantic Fidelity Project.

The collection focuses on what happens to meaning as representations are compressed, transformed, reused, and circulated through increasingly complex systems.

The five concepts form a connected structure:

- Constraint Collapse describes what happens when correction remains visible but loses binding force.
- Semantic Fidelity describes how well meaning, intent, context, and referential structure survive transformation.
- The Drift Principle explains why representational change can outpace real-world correction.
- Semantic Drift describes the degradation or alteration of meaning across repeated transformation.
- Recursive Compression explains how prior representations become inputs to later representations.

Together, these concepts describe how meaning can remain coherent on the surface while becoming progressively less grounded underneath.

> Coherence is not contact.

---

## 01_Constraint_Collapse

Constraint Collapse describes the condition in which feedback, consequence, and correction remain present but lose their ability to enforce change.

The problem is not that feedback disappears.

A system may continue detecting errors, producing reports, updating metrics, and acknowledging contradiction while losing the ability to invalidate its own representations or stop a misaligned process.

Within the Semantic Fidelity Project, Constraint Collapse matters because meaning can continue degrading even when evidence of that degradation remains visible.

Folder: `01_Constraint_Collapse`

---

## 02_Semantic_Fidelity

Semantic Fidelity describes the degree to which a representation preserves the meaning, intent, context, distinctions, and referential relationships necessary for its intended use.

It extends beyond simple factual accuracy.

A representation can contain accurate statements while still distorting the significance, hierarchy, intent, or function of the source.

The central question is:

Did the meaning survive the transformation?

Folder: `02_Semantic_Fidelity`

---

## 03_Drift_Principle

The Drift Principle states:

> Representations can be compressed, optimized, distributed, and reused faster than real-world conditions can correct them.

This principle provides the broader structural basis for the Semantic Fidelity Project.

As representations move faster through systems, they can be transformed many times before their relationship to the original referent is tested again.

The result is a growing gap between representational speed and corrective capacity.

Folder: `03_Drift_Principle`

---

## 04_Semantic_Drift

Semantic Drift describes the progressive alteration or degradation of meaning as a representation moves across contexts, systems, summaries, incentives, or repeated reuse.

The representation may remain recognizable.

The language may remain fluent.

The output may still appear correct.

What changes is the relationship between the representation and what it originally meant.

Semantic Drift therefore focuses on meaning loss that can remain hidden beneath surface coherence.

Folder: `04_Semantic_Drift`

---

## 05_Recursive_Compression

Recursive Compression describes the process by which systems reduce complexity into simplified representations, preserve those representations, and reuse them as inputs to later cycles of interpretation or action.

This process makes scale and cumulative knowledge possible.

It also creates a structural risk.

Later representations may increasingly depend on earlier compressed representations rather than renewed contact with the underlying source.

When fidelity is preserved, recursion can deepen understanding.

When fidelity is not preserved, recursion can compound loss.

Folder: `05_Recursive_Compression`

# How the Five Concepts Fit Together

The concepts can be read as a single representational process.

Reality  
↓  
Compression  
↓  
Representation  
↓  
Reuse  
↓  
Recursive Transformation  
↓  
Semantic Fidelity Preserved or Lost  
↓  
Semantic Drift  
↓  
Correction or Constraint Collapse

Recursive Compression explains how representations persist and become inputs to later transformations.

Semantic Fidelity describes whether meaning survives those transformations.

Semantic Drift describes what happens when that fidelity degrades.

The Drift Principle explains why these transformations can accumulate faster than reality can correct them.

Constraint Collapse describes the point where feedback remains present but loses the power to stop or reverse the drift.

# Core Questions

This collection is organized around a small set of questions:

- What survives when information is transformed?
- What is lost when complexity is compressed?
- How many transformations can occur before meaning changes materially?
- When does reuse become recursive distortion?
- How can information remain fluent while becoming less faithful?
- What keeps representations answerable to their sources?
- What happens when evidence of drift no longer forces correction?

---

# Relationship to Reality Drift

The Semantic Fidelity Project focuses on the meaning layer of Reality Drift.

Reality Drift describes the broader condition in which representations lose corrective contact with reality while systems continue functioning.

The Semantic Fidelity Project examines how that loss propagates through language, context, interpretation, and recursive representation.

The five concepts in this directory provide the core architecture for that analysis.
