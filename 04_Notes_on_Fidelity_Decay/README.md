# Notes on Fidelity Decay

This directory contains applied notes on how meaning weakens as information is transformed, compressed, retrieved, summarized, and regenerated by AI systems.

Semantic fidelity refers to the degree to which a transformation preserves the meaning, context, constraints, relationships, uncertainty, and intent of its source. A representation can remain fluent, plausible, or factually accurate while still changing what the original information meant.

These notes examine the specific mechanisms through which that loss occurs.

## Included Notes

### ChatGPT Sounds Right but Wrong

Examines how language-model outputs can remain coherent and well structured while losing alignment with the meaning or intent they were supposed to preserve. The failure is not always obvious factual error. Structure can survive while meaning shifts.

### Why Can an AI Summary Be Factually Accurate and Still Be Misleading?

Explores the difference between sentence-level accuracy and document-level fidelity. Summaries can preserve true statements while changing emphasis, scope, uncertainty, attribution, or the relationships between claims.

### How Does Abstraction Cause Information to Lose Its Meaning?

Describes how selection, compression, normalization, recontextualization, reconstruction, and recursive reuse can remove the relationships and constraints that gave information its original meaning.

### Semantic Fidelity, Meaning Preservation, and AI Interpretation Failure

Provides the broader conceptual framework for semantic fidelity and explains why factual accuracy, semantic similarity, and fluency are not sufficient measures of meaning preservation.

### Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Grounding, Faithfulness, and Reality Drift

Examines how AI systems can retrieve the correct source while still distorting its meaning during ranking, context selection, compression, interpretation, and generation. Access to evidence does not by itself guarantee fidelity.

## Core Pattern

Across these notes, the same structural problem appears:

**source meaning → representation → transformation → usable output → weakened fidelity**

Information remains available. Outputs remain coherent. Citations may remain present. Individual statements may remain correct.

What changes is the relationship between the representation and the meaning it was supposed to preserve.

Fidelity decay becomes especially difficult to detect when the output continues to look normal. Small changes in context, scope, certainty, emphasis, or implication can accumulate across repeated transformations without producing an obvious failure.

## Relationship to Reality Drift

Semantic fidelity is one of the constraints that keeps representations answerable to their sources.

Reality Drift describes the broader condition in which representations remain coherent, useful, and operational while becoming progressively less aligned with the realities they were created to represent.

Semantic fidelity focuses specifically on whether meaning survives transformation.
